# Profile — PDE Introduction Archetypes

These are conditional architecture aids, not paragraph templates. Select one primary PDE architecture and, at most, one secondary architecture when the theorem genuinely has two levels.

## 1. Equation–regime–frontier–mechanism

Use when the main issue is an endpoint, critical/subcritical/supercritical regime, or a sharply defined class of equations.

Required reader inferences:
- what the regime division is and why it matters;
- where the known theory stops;
- the exact endpoint or regime reached here;
- the control that makes the new regime accessible.

Keep the regime map compact. Do not survey every exponent studied in the literature.

## 2. Threshold or dichotomy

Use when a ground state, conserved quantity, scaling-critical norm, sign, or threshold separates scattering/global behavior from blow-up or another regime.

Required reader inferences:
- the threshold object and its normalization;
- the two sides of the dichotomy;
- why the threshold is sharp or natural;
- which assumptions enter compactness and which enter rigidity.

Do not state only the favorable side if the theorem's significance comes from the full threshold geometry.

## 3. Regularity, free boundary, or stratification

Use when the result decomposes points or solutions into regular/singular/generic/anomalous classes, or when the main theorem is a partial-regularity package.

Required reader inferences:
- the model blow-ups or tangent objects;
- the stratum being improved;
- the regularity or dimension conclusion for each branch;
- known examples establishing optimality or preventing a stronger statement.

A short definition stack before the theorem is legitimate when the branches are otherwise unreadable.

## 4. Stability, damping, or scattering

Use when linear dynamics predicts decay or asymptotics but nonlinear interactions threaten the prediction.

Required reader inferences:
- the linear mechanism and its natural norm or coordinates;
- the transient growth, resonance, loss, or nonintegrable effect that obstructs nonlinear closure;
- the custom energy, coordinate, normal form, monotonicity, or spacetime estimate used to close the bootstrap;
- the actual asymptotic state and topology of convergence.

Do not confuse linear intuition with a nonlinear proof overview.

## 5. Global regularity through a continuation route

Use when the theorem upgrades local well-posedness to global existence or smoothness.

Required reader inferences:
- the local theory and continuation criterion;
- the critical quantity whose control prevents breakdown;
- the estimate or modulus that controls it;
- which supercritical or rougher regime remains open or is known to fail.

Do not announce “global regularity” without locating the equation in its regime map.

## 6. Singular limit or geometric bridge

Use when PDE solutions converge to a surface, varifold, measure, free boundary, effective equation, or variational object.

Required reader inferences:
- the object dictionary across the two theories;
- the topology and scaling of convergence;
- how multiplicity, concentration, defect, or index is controlled;
- which result is intrinsic to the PDE framework and which is an application after transfer.

Use a two-audience bridge only to the depth needed for the theorem.

## 7. Solution-class separation and nonuniqueness

Use when existence, uniqueness, or nonuniqueness changes with weak/strong/admissible/Leray/entropy/energy solution concepts.

Required reader inferences:
- the precise constructed or controlled solution class;
- the scaling or admissibility benchmark;
- what stronger class is not reached;
- whether the limitation is a known obstruction, an open problem, or only a feature of the method.

Never let the headline “nonuniqueness” silently migrate to a stronger solution class.

## 8. Fully nonlinear or structural regularity

Use when regularity depends on convexity, concavity, coefficient regularity, ellipticity, or a structural condition on the operator.

Required reader inferences:
- the classical regularity benchmark under stronger structure;
- a counterexample or obstruction showing why the desired regularity is nonautomatic;
- the exact partial-regularity or dimension conclusion;
- the assumption that excludes important operator classes and why it enters the proof.

## Selection rule

Choose the architecture that determines what the reader must know before the theorem. Do not choose by subfield label alone. The same equation can support a threshold paper, a nonuniqueness paper, a stability paper, or a singular-limit paper, and each requires a different Introduction logic.
