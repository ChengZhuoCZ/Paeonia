# Grand Mathematical Introduction Profile

## Register

Broad in mathematical scope, ambitious in verified positioning, precise in claims. Grandeur comes from the theorem's place in a larger mathematical program, the structural transition it resolves, the historical/conceptual frontier, theorem strength, and mathematical consequences—not from adjectives.

## Parent-program geometry

For `EXPANSIVE`, `GRAND`, and `GRAND_PLUS`, first ask whether the focal theorem belongs to a larger mathematically operative program. When supported, build:

`focal theorem → focal problem → parent program → canonical principle`.

Then identify one primary structural motion, such as:

- continuum → finite complexity;
- limit/asymptotic → exact finite;
- local → global;
- qualitative → quantitative;
- symmetry → rigidity;
- compactness → exact structure;
- subcritical → endpoint;
- partial regimes → complete classification.

This is the mathematical story. A field name is not a story.

## Narrative geometry

Preferred when supported:

`canonical principle / parent program → exact scale transition → pre-paper frontier → structural obstruction → focal theorem → program-level interpretation → proof mechanism`.

The internal story graph may be richer than this. Publication prose should follow one critical path, not reproduce every related theory.

## Opening

Begin with the largest mathematically operative relation that leads naturally to the theorem. Good openings may start from a canonical principle, an unrestricted benchmark theorem, a local/global gap, a limiting/exact gap, or a family of parallel extremal problems.

Avoid generic `long history`, `fundamental area`, or routine functional-definition openings when the theorem belongs to a larger verified program.

## Literature

Use citations as mathematical ancestry. Group by role such as program origin, canonical theorem, restricted formulation, asymptotic/local frontier, global frontier, rigidity precursor, or obstruction evidence. Chronology is secondary.

## Parallel theories

A neighboring problem belongs only if it exposes a shared structure and a decisive difference. It should change how the reader understands the focal theorem, not merely enlarge the bibliography.

## Problem tension

Prefer the transition that the old theory cannot cross. Examples include continuous symmetry versus finite-complexity admissibility, weak convergence versus exact combinatorial structure, local coercivity versus global degeneration, or limiting rigidity versus a finite-parameter gap.

## Result presentation

Allow a theorem ladder only when the paper genuinely contains distinct structural, quantitative, global, or rigidity levels. Reader order may differ from proof order.

After the formal theorem, interpret the result back at parent-program scale. Do not merely restate the theorem.

## Re-expansion

Re-expansion should answer what changed in the larger mathematical picture: an asymptotic selection became exact, a local theory became global, a canonical symmetry principle survived a restricted class, a parameter range closed, or a sharp stability law emerged.

## Proof story

Describe the mechanism that crosses the central story transition. A proof inventory is insufficient if it never explains why the previously missing passage is now possible.

## Scientific context

Scientific/physical context is independent. It may lead or enrich the Introduction, but a model-driven paper still needs a visible parent mathematical program when one exists. A pure-math paper may have a GRAND/GRAND_PLUS mathematical story with no scientific context at all.

## Calibrated strong-claim policy

Strong mathematical words are evidence-sensitive, not forbidden. `complete`, `sharp`, `first`, `global`, `resolves`, `settles`, and `closes` should be used when their exact scope is verified and central to the contribution. Do not weaken a verified strong result merely to sound cautious. Prestige adjectives such as `breakthrough` or `far-reaching` should be replaced by a concrete mathematical relation whenever possible.

For exceptional papers, `GRAND_PLUS` is available when theorem strength and verified literature justify program-level positioning.

## Research projection

If the style reference is a textbook, monograph, or another research paper, preserve only abstract narrative decisions. Do not copy topic sequence, citation sequence, theorem order, paragraph skeletons, or wording.
