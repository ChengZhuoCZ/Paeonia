# Protocol 04r — Read the manuscript as a demanding referee

## Scope and relation to writing

Use when the user requests referee-style analysis, submission-readiness assessment, or skill improvement based on an actual polished manuscript. Review the requested artifact before rewriting it. A request to analyze does not automatically authorize changing mathematical claims or launching a complete proof-or-disproof project. In an ordinary prose-only assignment, consult this protocol only when a concrete issue calls for it.

This is a diagnostic reading route, not an additional universal gate. Record the findings in the existing editorial notes or in the requested referee report. Do not create a new ledger per criterion. Distinguish an editorial assessment, a checked local issue, and a complete correctness audit. Prior byte-preservation checks establish fidelity to the source, not validity of the source. Compilation and file counts say nothing about significance or publishability.

## Identify what would justify publication

Reconstruct the paper's strongest defensible contribution in ordinary mathematical language. Separate the importance of the problem from what the method adds. A major theorem can justify a paper without an independent broad method, and a familiar formalism can become a substantial contribution through a new quantitative use. Do not require every manuscript to advertise both.

For the contributions that matter, distinguish:

- an established ingredient;
- an interpretation or specialization of an established framework;
- a strengthened estimate, new regime, or new compatibility result proved in the manuscript;
- a prospective use whose missing hypotheses have not been supplied.

Compare with the nearest operative primary source, including its weighted variants and normalization. A relevant citation does not by itself explain the difference. State what remains to be done after that source is applied. If the literature comparison is incomplete, mark the historical novelty unresolved; do not turn a plausible difference into a priority claim.

The front matter should let a referee locate the decisive advance and see where the body establishes it. The strongest sentence may concern a compatibility or a uniform estimate rather than the most striking formal identity. Express the corresponding assessment naturally, under Protocol 04p; do not score vocabulary.

## Broader relevance needs a usable mathematical object

For an asserted connection to another field, identify what its reader can actually take away: a theorem, quantitative bound, construction, or viewpoint. Recover its inputs, conclusion, normalization, range, and limitations from the manuscript. If such a result is buried in a long composite theorem, consider foregrounding or separating it without changing its hypotheses. Keep centering, positivity, fixed-versus-growing parameter assumptions, and symmetry quotients visible.

An illuminating reinterpretation may be worth explaining even when it produces no independent theorem. In that case state its explanatory value accurately. Do not manufacture transferability by adding applications, generality, or a new theorem name. If a broader theorem would require new mathematics, present it as a proposed research task, not an editorial fix.

## Separate understanding the plan from checking the argument

The introduction should explain why the strategy is plausible and why its major choices are needed. The body must let a specialist verify the decisive operations. These are complementary standards. A longer outline cannot repair an unspecified finite sum or an unproved estimate in a central lemma.

At a bottleneck described as cancellation, positivity, transport, or uniformity, inspect the corresponding proof passage. Ask what the specialist would have to reconstruct unaided. An elementary identity may explain the idea while leaving the difficult passage to the actual coefficients untouched. In that situation:

- keep the elementary mechanism in the outline;
- identify the obstacle to applying it, such as nonuniform sampling, moving constraints, boundary modes, or exact denominators;
- locate the quantitative argument that overcomes that obstacle in the body.

For a newly constructed central argument, finite sums, completion coefficients, index ranges, and boundary cases must be specified well enough to reproduce the claimed identity. An explicit formula or a fully determined construction may suffice; no demand for formula expansion everywhere is implied. A standard imported estimate may remain compact once its applicable form and hypotheses are clear.

If the source itself omits the needed construction, report a proof-presentation concern or mathematical check. Do not invent the missing argument while polishing. If the user has authorized only prose edits, complete independent editorial work and preserve the unresolved issue in the report rather than stopping the whole task.

## Check the interfaces actually used

For a selected high-impact step, compare the invoked conclusion with its stated hypotheses and the information available at the point of use. Check the relevant distinction, not a universal checklist: periodic versus zero winding, centered versus uncentered, exact versus linearized constraints, all configurations versus minimizers, absolute versus relative errors, or fixed-parameter versus uniform estimates.

An apparent mismatch requires a bounded follow-up read: the hypothesis may be established nearby, the needed subclaim may require less, or a correction may already be included. State which possibility remains unresolved. Do not label a theorem false merely because an interface is not explicit. A conclusive error claim requires its own verified argument; a full proof audit is a distinct scope.

Check the noun phrase making the claim as well as the adjacent formula. A normalized quantity, a difference from a reference configuration, or an identity in an auxiliary model can have a correct formula and still be described as a different object. A quick check at the reference configuration often exposes that mismatch. Likewise, a sign-sensitive angular or gauge notation must select the representative used by the proof. These are targeted semantic checks when the passage calls for them, not a mandatory checklist of particular formulas for every paper. Local wording should carry the corrected meaning; a later general caveat is not always enough.

## Allocate explanation by the cost of the reader's inference

Read the abstract, conceptual announcement, detailed outline, and section openings together. Each repetition should add a different level of information. A concise top-level argument and a more technical explanation can coexist without a fixed page quota. Avoid making the broad reader retain all auxiliary symbols before learning the purpose of the argument.

Choose where a model or representative calculation would remove the actual uncertainty. It must preserve the obstacle being explained. A uniform-grid model cannot explain how an uncontrolled mesh becomes regular. It may illustrate the subsequent local comparison if that assumption and the separate regularity step are explicit. Do not prescribe a model, diagram, or additional subsection for every manuscript.

Likewise, do not cut a long proof merely because it is long. Compress routine dependencies and repeated summaries; expand a bottleneck when the reader would otherwise have to supply the central new proof.

## Turn the diagnosis into an authorized revision

When the user asks to revise after a referee analysis, use the actual objections to select edits. Before drafting, distinguish a repair available from the supplied mathematics, a claim needing further source checking, and a missing mathematical argument. Record this disposition briefly in the existing revision note. A proposed improvement in a review is not evidence that its mathematical content has already been established.

For a claimed omission, compare the current draft with the earliest supplied source and inspect nearby definitions and appendices. Restore material lost in editing when the source supplies it. If the source is equally terse, identify that provenance and do not manufacture a completion. Continue with the supported revisions; report the remaining issue with a precise locator and the evidence needed to resolve it. Do not describe all referee objections as addressed because the document was rewritten.

To foreground an existing result for another audience, extract its exact hypotheses, parameter regime, normalization, and conclusion from the body. A short introductory restatement may be useful when it gives readers an independently understandable mathematical result; identify its source theorem and retain that theorem and proof. Check the restatement against the original, including centering, positivity, asymptotic sequences, symmetry choices, and constant dependencies where relevant. Do not turn a sequential statement into an unstated fixed-parameter neighborhood theorem, or promote a useful viewpoint into a theorem merely to increase impact. An explanatory paragraph may be the better choice when a restatement adds no new reader access.

Revise the contribution narrative around what is demonstrably available. Existing frameworks belong near the operation they explain; the target's additional estimate or compatibility belongs near its proof obligation. Avoid both unsupported priority language and a catalogue of disclaimers in the article. Detailed unresolved checks belong in the revision report, without implying that the manuscript has received a complete correctness audit.

Finally reread the revised passages against the original objection: can the reader now recover the missing distinction, construction, or implication? Show a source-backed repair or retain an unresolved status. This is an editorial judgment about the actual text, not a keyword test. Preserve successful parts of the prior version, and do not add a prescribed number of headings, theorems, examples, or new references.

## Report a diagnosis that can change the next edit

Lead with a calibrated overall assessment. State the scope of inspection and avoid predicting a journal's acceptance decision or equating editorial polish with proof certification. For each material objection provide a precise locator, evidence, consequence, and the smallest justified next action. Distinguish:

- a visible exposition or definition problem;
- a literature/novelty question;
- an unresolved mathematical interface;
- a confirmed local error, if separately established;
- an optional presentation preference.

Prioritize by consequence for assessing the paper, not by how easy the edit is. State what evidence would resolve each important objection. Retain successful passages and identify their function; do not reverse a previous edit solely to create novelty in the revision report.

When improving the skill, infer a rule from the reason the manuscript failed, not from its exact vocabulary or subject. Put target-specific examples in the [referee case study](../references/referee_case_large_n.md). Preserve reviewer mode as optional, reuse existing diagnostics, and reconcile conflicting instructions instead of adding another competing workflow. A plausible new rule is not an independently validated behavior change; report what was actually tested.
