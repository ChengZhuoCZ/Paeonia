# Protocol 04p — Explain the reformulation and the proof

## Activation and priority

Use when a research Introduction contains a nonroutine change of viewpoint, several interacting proof mechanisms, a bootstrap, or a user request for a comprehensible proof outline. Apply after reading the operative proof, before Introduction paragraph planning. For an Introduction-only assignment, an existing supplied proof is enough in `trusted_source` mode; do not rewrite the body merely to unlock this pass. When only improving this skill, use supplied manuscripts as diagnostic evidence and leave them unchanged unless rewriting was requested.

This protocol qualifies all older instructions to compress pedagogy, delete reader coaching, avoid formula paraphrase, or make a proof seem inevitable. Compress familiar background and redundant narration. Preserve explanations of new objects, causal transitions, and the work done by a formula. A specialist has field knowledge, not advance knowledge of this paper's mechanism.

Its supported-judgment and method-connection rules also qualify older calm-tone, anti-adjective, and grand-introduction-only defaults. They apply whenever the user's scope calls for them; no higher introduction-scale label is needed.

The empirical reference is [the Naber corpus profile](../profiles/naber_introduction_and_outline.md), supported by [the source inventory](../references/naber_corpus.json). These are abstract explanatory decisions observed in papers by Naber and coauthors, not an author persona. Do not imitate wording, transfer topic-specific tools, or copy an article's paragraph order. Consult the [polygonal diagnostic](../references/large_n_reformulation_diagnostic.md) only for similar finite-configuration or relative-defect problems.

## 1. Diagnose what the reader cannot yet infer

Do not interpret “flat” as a request for more background, or “unclear outline” as a request for more stages. First locate the failure in the actual prose. A proof dependency graph records which results imply which; an explanation must also tell a reader why the chosen objects make those implications accessible. An outline can contain every lemma's input and output and still omit that reason.

Start from the audience's available knowledge. At each central transition ask what inference the next paragraph expects, whether the visible text enables it, and what smallest source-backed explanation would enable it. Read the proof to understand the decision, then return to the reader's state; do not count a fact remembered from the proof as something the outline has explained.

Common diagnoses lead to different repairs:

| Visible failure | Why it fails | Repair that changes the reader's inference |
|---|---|---|
| A representation is defined and immediately used | Its notation is available, but the reason for choosing it is not | Explain the simultaneous requirements it meets and the mathematical link that makes it useful |
| “Cancellation gives the desired bound” | The desired output is restated as if it were the mechanism | Identify what is recombined and why the surviving terms have the required sign, order, or small factors |
| Every technical module receives equal space | Logical coverage hides the conceptual center | Group routine work by its output and spend explanatory space on the few changes in what can be done |
| Strong theorem language, little methodological position | Readers learn what was solved but not what was learned | Identify the reusable relation or construction, then research its nearest mathematical relatives |
| An exhaustive outline remains unreadable | Definitions and deferred obligations exceed what the reader can track | Show the target assembly, introduce only its necessary objects, then zoom into the bottleneck |

For a difficult repair, keep a short private decision note: missing inference; source-supported reason; chosen explanatory move; why a simpler edit would not suffice; expected reader answer; when this move would be inappropriate. Use prose or the optional fields in the existing record. Do not require a new ledger per paragraph.

If the outline is readable but the decisive proof operation remains underspecified in the source, more introductory explanation is not the repair. In a requested review, use [Protocol 04r](04r_referee_perspective.md) to distinguish a missing definition or derivation from a prose problem. In prose-only work, record the concern without inventing mathematical content.

See [editorial reasoning cases](../references/editorial_reasoning_cases.md) for concrete distinctions, including cases where expansion should be rejected.

### Learn from a preferred result, not only from a failed draft

When the user identifies a successful passage, locate the decision that made it work: which objects became understandable, which calculation revealed the connection, and which question made the reader want the next paragraph. Preserve those functions when revising. A later fidelity correction does not require flattening the entire passage, and a successful tone does not validate its mathematical claims. The [conceptual-bridge profile](../profiles/conceptual_bridge_introduction.md) records the present user's positive example and its limits.

Avoid a common overcorrection: repeatedly adding every missing hypothesis and proof interface can produce a complete account with no discernible center. Keep the dependency record complete, but use it to decide what the prose must explain rather than as a sequence to transcribe.

For a preserved prose baseline, repair the clause that creates the missing inference while retaining its paragraph's entry question and closing implication. Start with a notation correction when that resolves the issue; if the words themselves misidentify a quantity or overstate a model, make the smallest authorized wording correction. A remote qualification may leave the original claim misleading. If the claim is frozen, report the conflict rather than silently treating an appended qualification as a complete repair. Do not rewrite a successful interpretation or outlook merely to make room for an audit. Keeping a passage unchanged is a valid polishing decision; new composition remains appropriate when the user requests it.

## 2. Find the conceptual hinge in the proof

Work backward from the result and locate the step that changes what can be measured, compared, constructed, or recovered. This may be a representation, relaxation, invariant, decomposition, gauge, dual formulation, or auxiliary problem. It need not be a new theorem in its own right.

Explain the choice as a mathematical design decision. Which constraints must the representation preserve, which analytic estimate must it fit, and which equality information must remain recoverable? If one object satisfies several previously disconnected requirements, their compatibility may be the main insight even when its definition is short. Distinguish the difficulty of discovering that compatibility from the length of verifying it. Do not invent discovery history or a failed alternative: a counterfactual route must be supported by the proof or explicitly presented as an expository comparison.

For each central reformulation record, briefly:

- **Original object and obstruction:** the concrete target and the missing information in a natural approach.
- **New object and dictionary:** what its components mean in the original problem; which admissibility conditions survive; which symmetries must be fixed.
- **Status of the bridge:** exact identity, equivalence, one-way reduction, principal-order correspondence, or comparison with a controlled remainder. Use the right status at every arrow.
- **New capability:** the positivity, compactness, symmetry, summability, coercivity, or recoverability made accessible by the change.
- **Price still to pay:** reconstruction, realizability, nonlinear errors, constraints, parameter uniformity, or equality recovery.
- **Proof anchors:** source labels supporting the bridge, the new capability, and the remaining obligations. Reading for exposition is not an independent proof audit.

A symbol definition is not an explanation of the reformulation. Nor does a formal encoding automatically identify an equivalent optimization problem. Do not claim every auxiliary configuration is realizable, or that the auxiliary energy equals the original functional, without the corresponding source result.

## 3. Give the contribution its earned prominence

Distinguish three judgments:

1. **Structural importance:** what the change enables in this proof; supported by the proof dependencies.
2. **Mathematical difficulty:** what must be established to make that change useful; supported by the actual bridge and error estimates.
3. **Historical novelty:** what earlier literature did not contain; requires literature verification.

The first two may be explained decisively without claiming the third. If a central reformulation is supported, give it a clear conceptual announcement and a dedicated explanation. Do not bury it between routine normalizations or refer to it only as an encoding. Terms such as “the central reduction” or “the main conceptual step” are appropriate when supported; adjectives alone do not demonstrate importance.

Make the advance visible through a specific change in mathematical access. Explain why an apparently convenient notation carries the information the initial method loses. If the same reformulation has several indispensable uses, make those uses distinguishable. Do not force an independent theorem statement merely to dramatize a method.

### Express mathematical judgment

Choose the kind of judgment before choosing its wording: an unexpected relation between objects, a striking consequence, a revealing reformulation, or an economy in which one construction resolves several obligations. These are different assessments, not interchangeable intensifiers. Give the judgment an identifiable mathematical referent and explain it nearby. A relation deserves emphasis when the original formulation conceals the bridge; elegance may lie in compatible roles or an argument saved, without implying that the remaining proof is easy.

Treat user examples such as “a surprising connection” as indications of expressive range unless the user explicitly requests those exact words. No phrase, synonym list, or sentence frame is mandatory. Wording may use an apt adjective, a precise verb, or a sentence that brings out the mathematical tension without an evaluative adjective. Choose what fits the object, surrounding prose, and strength of the evidence; do not replace mechanical phrase repetition with mechanical synonym rotation.

When a more expressive Introduction is requested, make the contribution's character and significance felt in the finished prose. Merely permitting adjectives in an internal rule is insufficient, but including an example phrase is not evidence of success either. Review the abstract and Introduction together for repeated rhetorical announcements; repeat an assessment only if the new passage develops a distinct point. Do not impose adjective quotas or automatically neutralize supported judgment during compression. Historical assertions such as “first” and “unprecedented” still require separate verification.

### Research the method's mathematical neighbors

When the user asks for broader relevance, or a central reformulation has an identifiable mathematical structure beyond the target problem, search by that structure before searching only by the conjecture's name. Useful search objects include the kernel or multiplier, dual norm, positivity certificate, equality configuration, representation, or transport operation actually used. Consult primary sources and inspect the operative statement or formula, not just a shared keyword or abstract.

Classify each retained connection:

- **Established ingredient or precedent:** the source contains the relevant construction or result; state its scope and cite it. Do not infer that the manuscript's author was historically influenced by it.
- **Exact reinterpretation:** show the dictionary and normalization that identify the target quantity with an established object. Attribute any new specialization to the present analysis, not to a source that never mentions the manuscript.
- **Structural analogy:** identify the matching operation and the important mismatch in hypotheses, variables, weights, or conclusions.
- **Prospective extension:** explain which parts would transfer and which input is unproved. It is a direction, not a theorem or an established application.

Retain a connection only if it explains the choice of formulation, makes a proof mechanism accessible to another audience, or identifies a concrete reusable contribution. Prioritize the nearest operative connection over prestigious but distant associations. Check precedents for weighted versions, normalizations, diagonal terms, admissible kernels, and equality cases before claiming novelty. A connection is not a reduction of the full problem unless every bridge has been established.

Introduce the shared mathematical question in accessible terms, give the minimum dictionary, and return to the target proof's extra difficulty. This lets neighboring specialists enter the argument without turning the Introduction into a survey. Methodological breadth can supplement a conjecture-resolution theorem without inventing a more general theorem. Use existing story/narrative notes for the research; no new governance layer is needed. The [polygonal connection dossier](../references/large_n_connections.md) is a case study, not a required literature list for other papers.

## 4. Build a route the reader can traverse

### Choose the conceptual explanation before expanding the route

For a paper organized around a reformulation, first decide what relation the reader should retain and which source-backed identity, calculation, or construction makes that relation visible. Draft a continuous explanation of the original objects, the bridge, and the new capability. Make clear which comparison is only principal-order and what it leaves unresolved. If the central insight needs an equation, do not replace it with several sentences naming advanced techniques.

Then select the resolution of the proof outline. Its job is to show how the remaining obstacles are overcome, with enough mechanism to make the crucial gains intelligible. Group routine stages by their shared output. Split two uses of the same named tool when the first supplies assumptions for the second. A proof with many lemmas need not produce an equally segmented Introduction.

This is a compositional option for a genuine conceptual center, not a universal order. A direct argument or a paper whose principal contribution is a quantitative estimate may be better explained by forward reasoning or conditional assembly from the outset. The private evidence and fidelity checks still precede claims; this choice concerns what the reader meets first.

Choose explanatory order from the reader's dependencies, not from the order of lemma numbers. Two useful options are:

- **Forward route:** available information → obstruction → new ingredient → improved information → next obligation.
- **Conditional assembly:** state an intermediate result with its meaningful assumptions and output; show how that output delivers the main theorem; then explain the hard work needed to establish it.

Use the second when readers otherwise cannot see why a long technical module matters. Declare what is temporarily assumed and return to it. This is exposition order, not a license for circular proof dependencies.

Keep the route short enough to remember but long enough to expose every indispensable bridge. No fixed number of steps, paragraphs, subsections, equations, or pages is prescribed. Subheadings are useful when they name mathematically distinct tasks; neither prose-only nor checklist-only structure is mandatory.

For each indispensable change in the argument, the reader should be able to recover:

- what is already known on entry;
- what is still unavailable;
- the ingredient being applied and why it fits;
- what it produces, including the relevant regime or uniformity;
- where that output is used next.

These are internal planning questions, not five sentence slots or a requirement to expose every intermediate lemma. Explain routine stages briefly; spend space on changes of viewpoint and bottlenecks. An assumption that changes the claimed scope remains explicit, while an auxiliary definition used only inside a routine estimate usually belongs in the body.

Choose how much of a module to open by the reader's task. A routine imported estimate may remain a black box once its hypotheses match the available information. A central newly constructed mechanism needs at least one visible reason it succeeds: an identity, a model preserving the obstruction, a geometric construction, or a precise matching of estimates. “This bound is useful next” explains its purpose, but not how the method obtains it. Do not expand every proof; open the box whose unexplained operation blocks comprehension.

## 5. Introduce objects before using their technical names as explanations

At first substantive use, explain a new object through its mathematical job. A reader should know what is being transported before encountering “material coordinates,” what quantity cancels before hearing “cyclic cancellation,” and which conclusion smallness permits before a “critical scale” is invoked.

Use a minimal dictionary for novel objects. Do not overload the first overview with every auxiliary norm, parameter, remainder symbol, or theorem number. Technical names can follow an operational description and remain useful navigation anchors thereafter.

Do not equate a term being defined with its role being understood. For a defect, explain what its zero set represents, what positive values measure, and which estimate converts its smallness to useful information.

Where a local outline already names the tool and its output, replace the opaque tool phrase with the operation on the relevant quantities before adding another explanatory sentence. Subtracting a common leading value, pairing two errors to retain a small factor, and transferring an estimate through quadratic coefficients are examples of operations, not required vocabulary. Keep any regime or model distinction on which that operation depends. Stop once the next inference is recoverable; replacing every technical term with a full derivation would defeat the outline.

After several local repairs, reread the resulting sentence as a whole. Correct conditions and concrete operations can still obscure the claim when nested inside an unfinished main assertion. If that is the actual reading burden, state the assertion with its scope, then explain how it is realized; preserve the causal direction and the scope of every qualifier. Split at a mathematical distinction rather than a word-count threshold. This is not a preference for uniformly short sentences, and it does not justify expanding an explanation that is already clear.

## 6. Keep the formulas that carry the mechanism

Include a formula when it explains the choice of representation, matches the output of one ingredient to the input of another, exposes a scaling mismatch, or closes the theorem. Introduce its objects and follow it with the implication the reader should retain. A short displayed derivation can be more economical than several technical nouns.

Prefer a linked derivation when it shows why an apparently unrelated quantity is forced by the original problem. Explain the operation responsible for the sign, exponent, or symmetry, rather than merely calling the resulting expression natural. Once the reader can see the relation, do not keep announcing it with new adjectives. Conversely, do not delete a concise conceptual announcement just because later formulas substantiate it.

Distinguish:

- redundant narration that only reads the symbols aloud;
- indispensable interpretation of what the estimate measures, why its sign/exponent matters, what regime it covers, and what it allows next.

The latter survives the compression pass. Conceptual formulas may be schematic if explicitly labeled; never silently replace an exact source identity or theorem by a heuristic equality. Avoid new constants and powers if they do not aid the mechanism.

## 7. Expose the obstruction that survives the reformulation

A useful new representation rarely finishes the argument. Explain the point at which the first plausible estimate stops being enough, then state what the stronger estimate must do.

For asymptotic-to-exact comparisons, keep two small quantities distinct: the external parameter and the competitor's defect. An additive error tending to zero in the external parameter need not be absorbable when the defect is still smaller. If the proof establishes a relative estimate, explain the need for an error coefficient multiplying that same defect, uniformly over the permitted competitors. Do not invent this mechanism for papers that do not use it.

For a bootstrap, expose the independent entry estimate, the improvement it provides, and the newly available hypotheses. Do not assume regularity at the stage whose purpose is to prove regularity. If an apparent loop is resolved by induction, different constants, separate regimes, or an initial coarse bound, say which resolution the source actually supplies.

For exact rigidity, explain how the estimates cover all positive defects in the stated class and how zero defect recovers the original object, including constraints and symmetry. Do not infer exactness from convergence alone.

## 8. Simplify transparently

A model case can remove incidental technical load while preserving the central obstruction. Specify the simplification, the feature it illuminates, and the source-backed route back to the general case. Do not call the restoration routine when it contains a main innovation.

For instance, assuming the unknown polygon is already regular or assuming the desired relative error bound would erase the very problem to explain. By contrast, a principal Fourier calculation may explain the exponent if it is labeled as a principal-order calculation and the exact coefficient comparison is identified as a separate obligation.

An intermediate lemma may be treated as a black box after its input, output, and purpose have been made clear. “Apply a technical lemma” is not a black-box explanation.

## 9. Compose, then test from the reader's side

If the proof is complex, add a `proof_explanation` record to `IntroductionParagraphPlan`, using [the schema](../schemas/proof_explanation.yaml). This remains an internal note; ordinary writing does not need a new visible governance workflow.

Compose original prose from the record. Do not serialize it. Then apply [Protocol 04q](04q_outline_cold_read.md) to the Introduction and outline alone. The final compression pass must preserve the explanations that made this read succeed; re-run the cold read after meaningful deletion.

If the same feedback returns, revisit the diagnosis and paragraph architecture rather than adding more sentences of the same kind. Prefer the shortest repair that restores the missing inference and communicates the earned judgment. Extra pages, named steps, source counts, or completed checks are evidence of work performed, not evidence of editorial success.
