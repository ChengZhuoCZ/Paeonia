# Protocol 04h — Narrative Ambition and Factuality Ceiling


> **Conditional positioning module.** Use only for a verified conjecture resolution, classification, first global result, sharp theorem, or a demonstrated under-positioning defect. Do not assign a grand scale merely to improve tone.

## Purpose

Determine how large the Introduction may legitimately think after the theorem position has been verified. When an operative larger mathematical program has been reconstructed, `MathematicalStoryLedger` is an input to this decision rather than an output invented by rhetoric; a sharp or complete theorem may trigger this module without requiring a parent-program story. The goal is not restraint for its own sake. The goal is to identify the **strongest defensible mathematical position** of the paper and preserve that strength without crossing the boundary set by the closed theorem package and verified literature.

This protocol produces `NarrativeAmbitionLedger`.

## Governing principle

The Introduction should use the strongest accurate description that the mathematics and literature support.

Do not weaken a verified strong result merely because strong wording feels risky. If the paper genuinely resolves a conjecture, gives the first global theorem in a specified class, closes the remaining parameter range, proves a complete classification, or establishes a sharp result, the Introduction should state that accurately.

Likewise, do not upgrade a result beyond what is verified.

The target is:

`maximal defensible scale under a factuality ceiling`.

## 1. Determine the mathematical scale

Classify the paper by the strongest applicable level:

- `STANDARD` — technically meaningful but primarily local or incremental;
- `EXPANSIVE` — substantial theorem, significant quantitative strengthening, or reusable method;
- `GRAND` — major conjectural, global, classificatory, or program-level advance;
- `GRAND_PLUS` — a result whose verified position justifies a particularly broad research narrative: for example resolving a central conjecture, completing a global classification, closing the last major range of a known program, establishing a first genuinely global result after a substantial partial theory, or introducing a mechanism with clear cross-problem reach.

`GRAND_PLUS` is exceptional. It is earned by mathematics and scholarship, never requested by tone alone. A broad parent program by itself does not justify `GRAND_PLUS`; the focal theorem must occupy a genuinely consequential position in that program.

## 2. Identify the strongest defensible position

Record every applicable position together with an evidence anchor:

- resolves a named conjecture;
- settles a previously open case or range;
- completes a classification;
- proves the first global result in a precisely stated setting;
- removes a historically meaningful restriction;
- reaches a sharp constant, exponent, asymptotic, or equality classification;
- unifies previously separate regimes;
- introduces a reusable proof mechanism;
- connects two previously separate lines of work in a mathematically operative way;
- materially strengthens the best prior result;
- provides a model result for a broader program.

No item may be stored without an anchor to either closed mathematics or verified literature.

## 3. Strong-word policy

Treat strong descriptors as **evidence-sensitive**, not taboo.

Words and constructions such as

`sharp`, `global`, `complete`, `first`, `resolves`, `settles`, `closes`, `best known`, `previously open`, `long-standing`

may be used when their content is verified and their scope is stated precisely.

Do not replace an accurate strong statement with vague underclaiming such as `we obtain a result related to...` merely to sound cautious.

Distinguish historical claims from supported mathematical judgment. Expressions such as `a surprising connection`, `unexpected`, `striking`, and `elegant` may orient the reader toward a specific relation or economy of argument. When the user asks for expressive positioning, use an appropriate supported judgment and explain its referent nearby; do not flatten it into neutral labeling. Protocol 04p gives the selection criteria. Broad labels such as `groundbreaking` or `far-reaching` still cannot substitute for the scope or consequence they imply.

## 4. Historical depth

Choose `SHORT`, `MEDIUM`, or `DEEP` historical depth according to how much history is needed to make the current theorem legible.

History earns space only if it clarifies at least one of:

- origin of the problem;
- conjectural line;
- strongest prior frontier;
- structural obstruction;
- why the present theorem closes or changes the picture.

A deep history section is not justified merely because the field is old.

## 5. Adjacent-field reach

Record adjacent areas only if the theorem or proof genuinely interacts with them. For each claimed broader reach, specify:

- shared mathematical structure;
- what the present result contributes;
- whether the relevance is theorem-level, method-level, or conceptual only;
- the sentence-strength ceiling allowed by evidence.

## 6. Methodological afterlife

Assess whether the proof mechanism has significance beyond the exact theorem.

Possible levels:

- `NONE` — method is problem-specific;
- `LOCAL` — likely useful for nearby variants;
- `TRANSFERABLE` — an identified mechanism plausibly applies to a broader family and this is explained mathematically;
- `PROGRAMMATIC` — the method supplies a new framework or reduction relevant to a larger program, supported by actual structural reasons.

Do not infer transferability from novelty alone.

## 7. Factuality ceiling

For every proposed amplification, record the strongest wording allowed by evidence and the stronger wording that is forbidden.

Examples:

- allowed: `resolves the conjecture for all convex N-gons`; forbidden: `settles the general spectral optimization problem`;
- allowed: `gives the first global result in this parameter range`; forbidden: `gives the first global result` if a larger scope is not verified;
- allowed: `the argument suggests a route to...`; forbidden: `the method applies to...` unless that application is established.

## 8. Output

Produce `NarrativeAmbitionLedger` containing:

- selected scale and reason;
- strongest defensible positions;
- evidence anchors;
- verified strong claims that should be surfaced;
- historical-depth budget;
- adjacent-area reach;
- methodological afterlife;
- factuality ceilings;
- claims that must remain weak or conditional.

This ledger is internal. It is not prose.
