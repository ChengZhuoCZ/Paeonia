# Protocol 04i — Calibrated Narrative Amplification


> **Not a default pass.** Apply only after identifying a concrete, evidence-backed under-positioning defect. Do not produce a second broader draft merely because the first draft is accurate or restrained. Protocol 04 forbids context expansion without a reader-facing need.

## Purpose

Strengthen an already accurate Introduction so that the paper is positioned at the largest defensible mathematical scale. This pass is editorial, not mathematical: it may improve context, contrast, obstruction, consequence, and method positioning, but it may not create new theorem claims or new literature facts.

Apply only after `IntroductionCoreBrief` and the factual draft are stable and a specific under-positioning defect has been recorded. Build `NarrativeAmbitionLedger`, `MathematicalStoryLedger`, `ScientificContextLedger`, or `IntroductionParagraphPlan` only when that defect actually requires the corresponding evidence. Scientific facts are researched in Protocol 04k, never invented during amplification.

## Allowed amplification operations

Every amplification must belong to one of the following classes.

### A. Broaden context through the parent program

Move from the exact theorem to the larger mathematical problem it genuinely inhabits, using the hierarchy and primary archetype recorded in `MathematicalStoryLedger`. Prefer a precise relation such as continuum→finite complexity, limit→exact finite, local→global, or symmetry→rigidity over generic expansion to a field name.

### B. Sharpen contrast

Clarify the strongest meaningful difference between:

- known and new;
- local and global;
- qualitative and quantitative;
- restricted and unrestricted;
- asymptotic and exact;
- existence and classification;
- theorem statement and prior numerical/conjectural evidence.

The contrast should be mathematical, not promotional.

### C. Clarify obstruction

Make the failure of the natural prior route more explicit when this explains why the theorem was genuinely inaccessible. Prefer structural reasons over generic difficulty language.

### D. Strengthen consequence

After the theorem, state the strongest verified way in which it changes the existing picture. Possible consequences include:

- closing a conjectural line;
- completing a parameter regime;
- converting a local/asymptotic picture into a global one;
- identifying the exact equality/rigidity structure;
- ruling out competing scenarios;
- providing a new benchmark or model case for a broader program.

### E. Expose methodological afterlife

Explain why the proof mechanism may matter beyond the exact theorem, but only at the strength certified by `NarrativeAmbitionLedger`.

### F. Re-expand to the mathematical program

After the theorem, make explicit the strongest verified way in which the result changes the parent program. This may convert an asymptotic picture into exact rigidity, globalize a local theory, complete a parameter range, or verify a canonical symmetry principle in a restricted class.

### G. Re-expand verified scientific context

When a `ScientificContextLedger` exists, reconnect the theorem to the verified phenomenon, model class, parameter regime, or mathematical-physics lineage. This operation may reorganize and foreground facts already present in the ledger; it may not introduce new scientific claims during prose amplification.

## Forbidden amplification

Do not:

- invent novelty or priority;
- turn a partial result into a complete one;
- widen theorem scope;
- claim a method applies to problems not actually connected by a demonstrated structural mechanism;
- create an open problem or historical narrative unsupported by sources;
- use prestige adjectives as substitutes for a concrete mathematical consequence;
- delay the main theorem merely to increase drama.

## Strong-result preservation

If `NarrativeAmbitionLedger` marks a verified strong claim as `must_surface`, the final Introduction must surface it at least once in clear prose or in the theorem orientation.

Examples include:

- exact conjecture resolution;
- first global theorem in a stated class;
- complete classification;
- sharp constant or equality result;
- closure of the remaining parameter interval.

Understatement of a verified central contribution is an editorial defect.

## Two-pass realization

### Pass A — factual draft

Write the Introduction so that every sentence is plainly supported by closed mathematics or verified scholarship.

### Pass B — amplification

Without adding new factual content, strengthen the narrative by applying only operations A–G above. Preserve or introduce a supported mathematical judgment when it identifies the concrete relation being explained, following Protocol 04p. If a broader connection has not yet been researched, return to the narrative research stage before writing it; an attractive analogy is not evidence supplied by amplification.

Then compare the two drafts semantically. Every new implication in Pass B must be licensed by the factual draft and the exact evidence artifact used for this repair. When no ambition ledger was triggered, the `IntroductionCoreBrief` and verified literature set the factuality ceiling.

## Output

Update the Introduction prose and record an `EditorialImpactAudit` showing:

- which amplifications were applied;
- which strong claims were surfaced;
- which tempting stronger claims were rejected by the factuality ceiling;
- whether the theorem's actual significance is fully represented.
