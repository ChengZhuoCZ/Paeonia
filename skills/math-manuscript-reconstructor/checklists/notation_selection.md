# Mathematical Notation Selection — Editorial Checklist

For each paper or editable section, inspect *actual* objects and important formulas. Do not construct a glossary merely to fill this checklist.

- [ ] Are main-theorem symbols conventional for the **specific field and problem**, rather than selected by one person's preferred alphabet?
- [ ] Do high-salience objects and active identities read naturally without frequent backtracking?
- [ ] Have genuinely contested high-impact choices been justified briefly in `NotationLedger.notation_selection.decisions`?
- [ ] Do related mathematical objects share a meaningful symbol family, and do unrelated objects avoid shared canonical visible symbols?
- [ ] Are sharp constants distinct from disposable bounds, and required dependencies displayed?
- [ ] Are the operator sign, eigenvalue numbering, Fourier transform, measure normalization, dimensions and boundary conditions preserved?
- [ ] Are generic vs specialized domains and ordinary vs symmetric/reduced/limiting configurations distinguished only where needed?
- [ ] Are indices assigned by meaning (vertices/modes/parameters/steps) rather than first appearance?
- [ ] Is every important symbol defined before its first use, without a large premature notation wall?
- [ ] Are proof-only temporaries confined to the proof rather than overloaded into the main theorem?
- [ ] Does each important exact-theorem citation have an explicit symbol crosswalk when conventions differ, checked against the actual version and locator?
- [ ] Did the operation preserve protected user notation and authorized editing scope?
- [ ] Was readability evaluated with representative formulas, not arbitrary numeric elegance scores or exact imitation of source authors?
- [ ] Did the structural checker pass, with its inability to certify editorial excellence or theorem applicability reported accurately?
- [ ] Were affected theorem statements, proofs, Introduction, references, appendices, and LaTeX macros rechecked after any substantive rename?
