# Protocol 04m — Contextual Grandeur and Grand Opening


> **Conditional module.** Use only when the theorem earns a program-level opening and that opening improves the exact theorem story. A strong journal target alone is not a trigger.

## Purpose

Prevent mathematically strong papers from receiving introductions that are factually correct but editorially small.

## 1. Grand opening test

For `GRAND` or `GRAND_PLUS`, ask before drafting the first paragraph:

1. What parent mathematical program or canonical principle makes this theorem inevitable to ask?
2. What scale/category transition separates the classical or limiting theory from the present exact problem?
3. Why is the present constraint/regime not a cosmetic variant?
4. What conceptual gap remains after all prior results?
5. What does the theorem change in that parent program, beyond closing the local statement?

If the answers exist but the opening begins with a routine definition or notation display, rebuild the opening.

## 2. Formula-first prohibition when context is earned

A formula may appear early, but a substantial GRAND/GRAND_PLUS Introduction should not begin by defining a standard functional merely because it is needed later. First establish the stake or the model unless the formula itself is the scientific phenomenon.

## 3. Mathematical-program arc

For ambitious papers, prefer a program arc such as:

`canonical principle → benchmark theorem → restricted/critical/finite setting → lost mechanism → exact frontier → present theorem`.

The arc may be historical, conceptual, or both. It must expose a mathematical transition, not merely age or prestige.

## 4. Historical arc

A deep historical arc may include:

`scientific origin → classical mathematical principle → conjecture → partial/local/computational theory → modern quantitative tools → present global theorem`.

Use only the segments that are verified and causally relevant.

## 5. Tension amplification

The strongest introductions often make one structural tension unmistakable. Examples include:

- continuous symmetry versus discrete admissibility;
- local coercivity versus global shape space;
- compactness versus loss of combinatorial structure;
- physical scaling versus mathematical singularity;
- microscopic model versus macroscopic PDE;
- symmetry of the limiting object versus exact rigidity at finite parameter.

The Introduction should name the actual tension of the paper and use it to organize the literature and proof story.

## 6. First-two-pages editorial test

By the time the main theorem becomes readable, a specialist editor should be able to recover:

- the parent mathematical program and canonical principle;
- the primary scale/category transition;
- its origin or scientific meaning when relevant;
- the exact pre-paper frontier;
- the structural obstruction;
- the theorem's scale;
- why the result is stronger than a limiting/asymptotic/local statement;
- what conceptual mechanism makes the proof possible.

If the Introduction merely supplies definitions, citations, and a theorem statement, it fails even when every sentence is correct.
