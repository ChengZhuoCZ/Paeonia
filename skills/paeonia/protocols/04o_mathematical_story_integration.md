# Protocol 04o — Mathematical Story Integration


> **Conditional integration module.** Use only after Protocol 04 has triggered mathematical-story research. The story may occupy one precise arc; no mandatory opening paragraph or program-return paragraph is imposed.

## Purpose

Integrate `MathematicalStoryLedger` into the Introduction so that the paper reads as part of a larger mathematical program rather than as a technical solution followed by retrospective context.

## 1. Story has priority over background taxonomy

When this module is triggered, organize the relevant part of the Introduction around the primary mathematical story before it is organized around:

- definitions;
- bibliography chronology;
- physical background;
- theorem numbering;
- proof section order.

Scientific context from Protocol 04k is a second axis. When present, it may lead or interleave with the mathematical story, but it does not replace the need to identify the parent mathematical program.

## 2. Canonical macro arcs

Choose the arc induced by the ledger. Typical forms include:

### A. Principle → restriction → failure → theorem

`canonical principle → unrestricted theorem → constrained class → why the principle no longer closes → present theorem`

### B. Limit → finite problem → exactness gap

`limiting optimizer/theory → finite-complexity approximation → asymptotic selection → why convergence is insufficient → exact finite rigidity`

### C. Local → global

`candidate and local theory → moduli/degeneration/global obstruction → compactness or replacement mechanism → global theorem`

### D. Family → missing member

`parallel extremal/classification family → known members → structurally distinguished unresolved member → present closure`

### E. Qualitative → quantitative

`classical inequality/existence → rigidity/equality → stability deficit → sharp quantitative theorem`

### F. Critical transition

`subcritical/regular regime → threshold phenomenon → failure of compactness/monotonicity → critical theorem`

These are diagnostic arcs, not prose templates. Never force the paper to visit every node.

## 3. First-page budget

When the verified larger story belongs on the first page, spend that scarce space on the largest mathematically operative relation available.

A strong first page often establishes:

1. the parent program or canonical principle;
2. the target restriction/regime;
3. the structural reason the target is not covered by the classical theory.

Only then should notation and the exact functional be introduced, unless the functional itself is the canonical principle.

## 4. History must change the mathematical state

Every historical paragraph must move the reader through a mathematical state transition, for example:

`conjecture formulated → solved in special class → local theory established → asymptotic theory obtained → exact global gap remains`.

A paragraph that only makes the subject look old or prestigious is removable.

## 5. Parallel problems as structural evidence

Use related functionals or neighboring conjectures to reveal a pattern, not to enlarge the bibliography.

A productive parallel paragraph should establish something like:

- the same regular/symmetric object is extremal for several continuum functionals but the finite-complexity analogue behaves differently;
- one member of an inequality family has a global theorem while another remains open because a specific structural identity is absent;
- a related classification problem has a mechanism that the present paper adapts in a nontrivial way.

The return sentence must state what the parallel changes about the present problem.

## 6. Theorem placement

A larger story is not permission to postpone the theorem indefinitely.

Once the reader knows:

- the parent program;
- the exact target class/regime;
- the pre-paper frontier;
- the central obstruction;

the main theorem should appear.

For most `GRAND` papers this still means within the first several pages, not after a mini-survey.

## 7. Re-expand only when program-scale interpretation is informative

After the formal theorem, interpret it back at the scale of the story ledger when that adds a non-obvious consequence. Do not add a ceremonial program-return paragraph merely to complete an arc.

Examples of valid moves:

- `the finite-complexity problem now matches the continuum symmetry prediction in the stated regime`;
- `the result converts a previously asymptotic selection principle into exact finite-parameter rigidity`;
- `the local theory is now globalized across the full moduli space`;
- `the theorem closes the last missing member of the stated family`.

Do not merely repeat the formula, and do not claim more of the parent program than the theorem actually resolves.

## 8. Proof story should explain the scale transition

The proof overview should answer why the larger-story gap can now be crossed.

For example, if the story is `LIMIT_TO_EXACT_FINITE`, the proof overview should identify the estimate or rigidity mechanism that resolves errors smaller than the finite gap. If the story is `LOCAL_TO_GLOBAL`, explain the compactness/degeneration mechanism that extends local coercivity globally.

A proof inventory that does not explain the story transition is inadequate.

## 9. Scientific-context merge

When `ScientificContextLedger` is active, choose one of:

- `MATH_LED_WITH_SCIENCE_ORIGIN` — scientific origin opens, then the mathematical program becomes primary;
- `INTERLEAVED` — scientific and mathematical histories genuinely co-evolve;
- `MATH_ONLY_MAINLINE` — scientific context is substantial but secondary to the mathematical program;
- `SCIENCE_LED_WITH_MATH_PROGRAM` — the model is primary, but the parent mathematical program is still explicitly identified.

Do not let physical motivation erase the mathematical ancestry of the theorem.

## 10. Cold-read program test

After drafting, a specialist reader who sees only the Introduction should be able to complete:

> This paper is not only about [local theorem]. It is about the larger problem of ________, and the precise missing transition is ________.

If no precise answer is possible, rebuild the Introduction narrative.
