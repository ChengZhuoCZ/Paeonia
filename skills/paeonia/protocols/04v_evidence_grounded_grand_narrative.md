# Protocol 04v — Evidence-Grounded Grand Mathematical Narrative

## Purpose and default

**Always test** a newly reconstructed Introduction for a genuine larger mathematical program; **write the largest honest narrative that improves the reader's understanding**, not the longest or most emphatic opening. This is a small default *editorial decision* inside the `IntroductionCoreBrief`, not a command to instantiate every Grand Introduction ledger. The rigorous research and fair-credit map from [Protocol 04u](04u_literature_discovery_and_credit.md) comes first.

When the theorem really belongs to a consequential parent program, a more expansive opening is the **default ambition** even if the supplied draft begins with a formula or local technical lemma. When no operative program connection survives verification, the output is a strong, specific, theorem-led Introduction—not an invented `GRAND` rating. `GRAND` and `GRAND_PLUS` labels require the demonstrated threshold in Protocol 04h and cannot be earned by venue, tone preference, or adjective choice.

## 1. Upward discovery: what larger question makes this theorem matter?

From the closed theorem and checked sources identify, without assuming all levels exist:

`CANONICAL_PRINCIPLE → MATHEMATICAL_PROGRAM → FOCAL_PROBLEM → EXACT_FRONTIER → PRESENT_THEOREM`.

Each arrow must be a **supported mathematical relation**, such as a restriction, a failed method under discretization, a transition from local to global, an endpoint, a sharp stability question, or a limiting-to-exact rigidity gap. Name the operation, invariant, object class, or quantitative mismatch that makes the arrow real. If you cannot explain an arrow in mathematical terms, delete it rather than attach a famous topic by association.

Useful core question:

> Why would a mathematician interested in the larger program recognize this particular theorem as a natural, nontrivial step, and what exactly changes after it?

## 2. Grand narrative is a causal argument, not ceremonial scale

A strong story has five interlocked components; they need not be five paragraphs:

- **Programmatic stake:** a recognized classical principle, question, model, or symmetry with a source-backed mathematical formulation;
- **Intellectual ancestry:** origin, key advances, and partial frontiers with named, fair credit to the work that actually accomplished each step;
- **Structural tension:** the precise reason an available general method/theorem does not descend to the target class or regime;
- **Resolution:** the exact theorem and its conceptual hinge, with no strengthened hypotheses/conclusion;
- **Return to the program:** a specific interpretation (e.g. a local-to-global bridge or an exact-rigidity strengthening), and explicit limitations or still-open settings.

Use one primary story. Alternative frames may be sketched internally, but do not splice several disconnected histories to sound broad. **Literature establishes the plot**: the historical story may not contradict the credit map or displace a close direct predecessor with a famous classical name.

## 3. Three scales, none attached to word count

- `DIRECT`: if the parent-program link is weak, lead with the exact problem and a striking, verified obstruction; do not force a historical panorama.
- `PROGRAMMATIC`: when origin/benchmark and a genuine restriction or transition are known, rise to the canonical principle and descend to the exact unresolved question. This is the preferred grand mathematical register for a substantial result.
- `HISTORICAL_SYNTHESIS`: when a multi-era or multi-branch program is indispensable, join source-backed historical developments into one causal chronology **without turning it into a roster**. Use detailed 04n/04o and 04h only when needed.

`DIRECT` is not synonymous with mathematically minor; `HISTORICAL_SYNTHESIS` is not evidence of major novelty. Choose after research and theorem assessment, not before.

## 4. Make the first two pages carry actual mathematical force

The first paragraph should begin from a concrete relationship, phenomenon, program, or problem pressure—not `This area has attracted much attention`, generic field importance, paper organization, or citation-heavy ceremony.

The opening may look historically broad but must quickly expose the **specific unresolved transition** relevant to the new theorem. The classical benchmark should be attributed where it is used. Literature should be threaded through the argument: each author or group has a distinct contribution, each transition creates a question, and that question forces the theorem.

`parent question → verified earlier achievement → remaining structural obstruction → main theorem → mathematical consequence`

This is a causal spine, **not a fixed number of paragraphs**. The main theorem appears at the earliest point a specialist can interpret it. Avoid delaying it to justify a prolonged origin story. The result's reach should be visible in a mathematically meaningful comparison, not an abstract declaration of importance.

## 5. Amplify mathematically before amplifying rhetorically

To make a draft genuinely more expansive, preferentially add **one verified edge**, not adjectives:

1. Explain why a classical mechanism fails under the new admissibility restriction.
2. Show why local or limiting information falls short of an exact/global conclusion.
3. Relate stability, rigidity, classification, or equality cases that change the theorem's meaning.
4. Compare genuinely parallel theories when their difference illuminates the obstruction.
5. Explain how the proof's central representation translates between two existing mathematical viewpoints.

Each proposed connection must name `source → exact structure shared → difference → relevance → claim strength ceiling`. Distinguish proved application from a plausible future program. An analogy never becomes an implication merely because the Introduction calls it a bridge.

## 6. The central contrast and conceptual turning point

Write down internally a single contrast, ideally in a form such as:

`known mechanism A controls class X, but target class Y violates indispensable property Z; the paper finds structural substitute B and thus proves C`.

If no such contrast exists, use the strongest correct alternative: a sharp threshold, new invariant, unexpected equivalence, exact classification, or strengthened quantitative bound. The proof overview should make `B` legible, not list arbitrary lemmas and numeric bounds. If the claimed novelty is purely technical, locate its genuine mathematical consequence without inventing a philosophical revolution.

## 7. Credit and prose integration

- Give **positive credit** to earlier authors and precise theorems before announcing the gap; avoid belittling the literature.
- Preserve distinctions among origin, exact ancestor, modern frontier, competing method, parallel result and survey.
- Use enough citations to let a specialist locate the claims, with no global `few citations` ceiling; remove citations whose role cannot be explained.
- Do not hide a mathematically closest paper beneath a sweeping sentence listing illustrious predecessors.
- `We settle`, `first`, `complete`, `optimal`, `long-standing`, and `unexpected` require the exact scope and appropriate research evidence. If a strong verdict is earned, do not euphemistically weaken it.
- Do not imitate paragraph order or characteristic wording from the cited papers, the optional author style corpus, or the supplied source Introduction.

## 8. Cold-read audit

A specialist, seeing only the Introduction, should be able to answer:

1. What **larger mathematical question** makes the present result natural?
2. Which authors/theorems originated and advanced the exact question, and what did they establish?
3. Where was the pre-paper frontier, on which axes, and why did it stop there?
4. Which **nonroutine barrier** separates the classical or partial work from this theorem?
5. What does this paper establish, exactly, and what is the conceptual turning point?
6. What changes in the larger mathematical picture, and which claims remain open?

Then apply the ordinary reader contract, swap/detachability/latency tests, Protocol 07 claim verification, and a `credit inversion` check: **Would a reader erroneously attribute a prior contribution to the present authors if the citations and nearby sentences were read literally?** Repair the weakest inference, not the entire Introduction by default.

## 9. Output and limits

Store a `programmatic_story` choice in `IntroductionCoreBrief`: selected scale, one parent principle if justified, exact tension, critical cited predecessors, claim ceiling, and program-level result interpretation. If no broader tie is supported, record that decision concisely. No extra ledger is required for an ordinary paper. Deep stories can still use Protocols 04n/04o, narrative ambition 04h, scientific context 04k/04l, and editorial 04j *conditionally*.

For `LOCAL_POLISH`, first identify the authorized paragraph or claim needing programmatic context; do not reconstruct surrounding effective prose or expand scope merely because the present protocol names an ambitious default.
