# Protocol 04s — Abstracts: causal compression and reference comparison

## Scope and reference roles

Use this pass for an abstract or an explicitly requested skill-application comparison. Apply the writing mode and protected boundaries first. Distinguish the accepted manuscript, a user-selected reference, and a new candidate. A user-provided abstract may be a frozen baseline, a positive example, or both. Permission to compare it does not authorize overwriting a protected version; a requested trial may be delivered separately. Do not ask again for a mode already selected in this continuing task.

Read the relevant result and proof interfaces as trusted writing evidence. This pass verifies the wording against them, not the correctness of the whole proof. Keep review notes outside the article.

## Recover the mathematical work before composing

Identify the exact result and the few inferences needed to understand what makes its proof substantive. Depending on the paper, these may include a limitation of existing information, a representation that recovers what was lost, an estimate that makes that representation useful, an obstruction that survives it, and the argument returning to the original conclusion. These are diagnostic questions, not required sentence slots or a universal narrative order.

Preserve theorem semantics before optimizing exposition: admissible objects, parameter regime, quantifiers, constraints, normalization, uniqueness and symmetry. Do not broaden a theorem about minimizers into a comparison for all nearby competitors. Do not turn a qualitative conclusion into a quantitative estimate, or discard an equality case to save a line.

For each mechanism selected for the abstract, ask what it acts on, what it produces, and why that output matters. Retain a technical term when it is standard for the audience or carries a useful distinction. A specialized name alone does not explain its work. Conversely, the abstract need not define every coordinate or reproduce the construction once the relevant action is clear.

## Compress relations, not only names

Prefer a small number of operative relations to a catalogue of modules. When a common quantity links the stages, let the reader follow its changing role: what it measures, how it enters the main estimate, and how it detects equality. Use that pattern only when the actual proof has such a quantity.

Check the direction and type of control. “Controls” may hide an upper bound, a lower bound, an equivalence or a coercivity statement. Resolve the direction when it matters for understanding the conclusion. The replacement must name the same object as the proof; a lower bound for a positive quadratic part is not yet a lower bound for the full nonlinear functional.

For an asymptotic argument with an exact conclusion, decide whether relative error or uniformity at vanishing defect is indispensable. If it is, preserve the distinction between a small error and an error small compared with the relevant defect. Do not import every cutoff, rate and bootstrap stage into the abstract. The Introduction should explain the mechanism at higher resolution.

Choose emphasis through the mathematical contrast or economy. A surprising relation can be named when supported, but neither an emphatic adjective nor its removal is a requirement. Avoid making the abstract a compressed audit or promising a general method that the paper has not established.

## Apply, freeze, compare

When a user requests skill improvement followed by application and further improvement:

1. Inspect the current instructions for a demonstrated decision problem; revise only if one is found. Save the actual skill version used before drafting the candidate. Repeated use does not itself require a new rule or manuscript change.
2. Apply it within scope. Preserve the actual candidate before adapting instructions to the outcome. If the completed review identifies no warranted edit, retain the whole candidate under [Protocol 04q](04q_outline_cold_read.md), record the concrete checks and rejected edits, and reuse unchanged public artifacts. Distinguish that completed editorial review from new prose generation and from earlier compilation or reader validation.
3. Compare the candidate with both the accepted baseline and the supplied reference. Identify a concrete reader inference gained or lost, the exact mathematical qualification affected, and the extra explanatory burden. Do not rank by length, technical vocabulary, sentence count or recency.
4. Revise only the instruction responsible for an observed decision problem. Report whether the actual manuscript kept the candidate, restored reference wording or adopted a bounded repair. A further skill revision is not evidence that a second manuscript application occurred.

If the user supplied the reference in advance, disclose the comparison as context-aware editorial review. Do not claim blind generation, independent comprehension, or general writing quality from a same-agent comparison. Record hashes or exact differences when they help distinguish stages; no large governance artifact is required.

## Refinement after an actual comparison

A missing implementation detail is not automatically a missing abstract inference. If the reader can identify the object, the required kind of comparison, and the conclusion it licenses, the coordinate construction may remain in the Introduction. Expand only the first inference that fails; do not convert every technical name into its own sentence.

Treat the ending as a return to the theorem. Keep causal roles and the original object distinct: a hypothesis may force an auxiliary conclusion, while a proved characterization identifies the extremizer. At abstract resolution, state this implication without automatically naming every supporting reconstruction. Preserve the source regime and any nonautomatic return, but do not repeat an equality configuration already identified. Choose one sentence or several according to the actual dependency, not a preferred template.

Compare at the level of the operation, not the whole candidate. Retain the reference's successful economy while accepting a precise directional or causal repair. A longer trial need not be accepted or rejected as a unit. Do not infer that fewer words are always better: deleting the relative-error requirement or the range needed for exact rigidity would destroy the argument's meaning.

The [causal abstract case](../profiles/causal_abstract_calibration.md) documents one such comparison and its limits. Read it as evidence for these decisions, not as a required six-sentence abstract or a vocabulary bank.

## Check at the appropriate resolution

Read the abstract by itself and recover the result, the decisive idea, and why the remaining difficulty does not invalidate the conclusion. Point to the words permitting those inferences. Consult the body afterward for fidelity. The Introduction may supply technical implementation, but it must not be needed to determine what the abstract is actually claiming.

Transfer the lesson to the paper selectively. At a proof bottleneck, expose a missing operation or reason; at a routine literature pointer, preserve brevity. A successful abstract is not a sentence template for every section, and not every local edit justifies a new rule.
