# Introduction Narrative Archetypes

These are selection aids, not paragraph templates. Choose one primary arc from the theorem and reader burden.

## Benchmark–restriction–gap

Use when a classical principle solves an unconstrained or limiting problem but a finite, discrete, critical, or geometric restriction destroys the original mechanism. The opening should make the benchmark and the lost mechanism visible; the theorem then answers the restricted problem.

## Conjecture–frontier–resolution

Use when a precise conjecture genuinely organizes the literature. State the strongest verified frontier and the exact part settled here. Do not retell the entire history.

## Problem–obstruction–theorem

Use for focused technical papers. Begin from the exact problem, show the nonroutine obstruction, and state the theorem early. This is the default when no larger story is needed.

## Limit–exactness

Use when asymptotic, local, or limiting information is known but exact finite/global rigidity is missing. Explain why convergence or localization does not identify the exact optimizer or classification.

## Phenomenon–formulation–explanation

Use when the theorem explains a striking phenomenon. The phenomenon must be mathematically precise; avoid anecdotal opening material.

## Representation–capability

Use when a new representation is itself central. Give the original object dictionary, the exact bridge, the new control, and the remaining proof cost. Do not present a notation change as a conceptual advance.

## Scientific model–regime–frontier

Use only when the model, scaling, or parameter regime changes why the theorem is natural. Return explicitly from the scientific description to the mathematical problem.

## PDE specialization

For PDE-governed papers, the primary narrative arc is supplemented—not replaced—by `pde_introduction_archetypes.md`. The generic arc answers how the story moves; the PDE architecture answers which theorem coordinates, branches, solution classes, and closure interfaces must remain visible.

## Selection rule

Prefer the arc that makes the exact gap and theorem intelligible with the least generic background. A stronger journal does not require a grander arc; a stronger theorem may require a broader one.
