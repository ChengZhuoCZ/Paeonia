# Grand Introduction Reference Distillation Report v2

This report records only abstract, transferable observations from the designated research-article Introduction. It contains no source sentences and is not a template.

## Narrative observations

- The Introduction repeatedly narrows from a broad spectral/geometric context to a polygonal extremal problem, then briefly widens when a neighboring Dirichlet theory clarifies the mathematical landscape.
- Adjacent material is useful only because it supplies a shared polygonal structure and a contrasting method regime; the narrative returns to the Steklov problem once that contrast has done its work.
- The obstruction is not left as a generic claim of difficulty: the nonlocal/boundary structure is used to explain why familiar local surgery, flow, or symmetrization mechanisms do not transfer directly.
- Results are not presented as a flat list. A large-parameter statement, an effective quantitative expansion, and a finite/global closure occupy distinct narrative roles.
- The proof overview is causal: a change of representation exposes symmetry, symmetry gives a structural reduction, the reduction enables a scalar/quantitative analysis, and finite verification closes the remaining regime.

## Prose observations

- Mathematical objects and results usually carry the paragraph, while literature serves to position them.
- Paragraphs often end by narrowing, exposing a gap, or creating the need for the next result rather than by giving a generic importance sentence.
- The Introduction allows longer literature/obstruction paragraphs and shorter theorem bridges; paragraph pressure is not uniform.
- A return from an adjacent theory is explicit at the level of mathematical subject, not merely chronological transition.
- The final proof overview is written as a mechanism before the paper organization is listed.

## Research projection

For use in specialist research articles:

- compress textbook-level definitions and physical motivation unless needed for the theorem;
- preserve the large-to-small mathematical movement and obstruction-driven transitions;
- retain literature only when it changes the theorem's position;
- do not inherit the reference paper's length, theorem count, citation order, or paragraph skeleton.
