# Profile — Literature-First, Fair-Credit, Programmatic Introduction

## This profile is a decision aid, not an imitation template

Writing energy is earned by mathematical ancestry, true conceptual stakes, and the theorem's exact advance. First search externally and check originals; then discover the structure that supports the broadest honest opening. Source passages are evidence, never sentence banks.

### Scholarship and narrative roles

- **Origin:** who first framed the precise question or created the operative model?
- **Canonical principle:** which established result predicts the phenomenon or supplies the benchmark?
- **Divergence:** what mathematical change of category/scale breaks the canonical proof?
- **Frontier:** which authors proved the most relevant partial or incomparable conclusions?
- **Hinge:** what relation or construction makes the present theorem possible?
- **Afterlife:** what exact view of the parent program is now supported; what remains outside scope?

A strong paragraph can contain multiple roles but must advance one mathematical inference. Named authors should be attached to achievements, never simply decorative citations.

### Opening selection

`PROGRAMMATIC` is preferred when a credible larger program explains the theorem. `DIRECT` is preferable when a broad frame would be artificial. `HISTORICAL_SYNTHESIS` is reserved for program history that materially changes the result's meaning. Do not decide from the journal target, author's preferences for prestigious language, or a fixed line count.

### Mathematically large rather than rhetorically swollen

Prefer to make the discontinuity between *what was known* and *what had to be proved* visible. A single well-supported exact-versus-asymptotic or local-versus-global contrast often yields more intellectual scope than several paragraphs of generic historical review.

### Credit integral to the plot

Every central pre-paper achievement should have a source and an accurate verbal assignment of its authorship or community lineage. When different papers address different axes, say so. Preserve independent parallel contributions and contemporaneous papers when relevant. If priority is disputed or a source cannot be checked, narrow the prose and report uncertainty.

### Adversarial deletion

Remove any historical or adjacent mathematical passage that does not alter the reader's inference about the theorem, obstruction, proof hinge, or long-range meaning. Grandeur survives deletion because it rests on mathematical relations.
